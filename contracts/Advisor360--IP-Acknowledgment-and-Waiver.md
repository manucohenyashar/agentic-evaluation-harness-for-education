# Advisor360 — Outside Project Disclosure, IP Acknowledgment, and Waiver

**Prepared for:** Emmanuel Cohen-Yashar
**Purpose:** To obtain written confirmation from Advisor360 that it asserts no ownership
interest in the Agentic Evaluation Harness project, and that the activity is permitted under
Emmanuel's employment agreement and company policy.

---

> **Read this first — not legal advice.** Have an employment attorney licensed in
> Massachusetts review both the letter below **and your actual employment paperwork** before
> you send anything. Massachusetts has no broad statutory carve-out equivalent to California
> Labor Code § 2870, so whatever your Proprietary Information and Inventions Agreement says
> is likely to govern on its own terms. A signed acknowledgment is cheap now and effectively
> unobtainable once there is a dispute or once MIT money has moved.

---

## Part 0 — Do this before drafting anything

**Find and read these documents.** The letter below has blanks that only your paperwork can
fill, and the right version of the letter depends on what you find.

| Document | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Offer letter | Any incorporation of a separate invention or confidentiality agreement |
| Proprietary Information and Inventions Agreement (PIIA), or equivalently named "Employee Invention Assignment," "Confidentiality and IP Agreement" | The **assignment clause** — does it reach inventions made outside work hours? Does it reach anything "related to the Company's business" or "anticipated research and development"? Is there a **holdover** clause covering inventions for N months after termination? |
| The same agreement's **Exhibit A / Schedule of Prior Inventions** | Whether you completed it at hire. If you did and this project isn't listed, say so plainly in the letter; if it was left blank, that is worth knowing before you assert anything |
| Employee handbook | **Outside Activities**, **Moonlighting**, **Conflict of Interest** sections, and any required approval process |
| Any non-compete or non-solicit | Massachusetts Noncompetition Agreement Act (2018) imposes real limits, but check what you actually signed |

**Two facts to establish for yourself before you write:**

1. **When was the design document authored, and on what equipment?** The MIT agreement's
   Recital A asserts it pre-dates that engagement. It does **not** assert it pre-dates your
   Advisor360 employment, and it probably doesn't. That means the design document itself must
   be inside the waiver — not just future work.
2. **Did any Advisor360 resource touch it?** Company laptop, company GitHub, company Claude
   or API account, company network, work hours. If the answer is yes anywhere, tell your
   attorney before you send the letter. It changes the ask from "acknowledge you have no
   claim" to "please waive a claim you may have," which is a different conversation and
   should be framed honestly as such.

**Send it to HR and Legal, not only your manager.** A manager's verbal "sure, that's fine" is
worth nothing eighteen months later. Only the entity can waive the entity's rights.

## Part 2 — The acknowledgment letter (for Advisor360 to countersign)

---

**ADVISOR360, LLC**
[Address]

**Date:** [DATE]

**Re: Outside Project — Acknowledgment of Non-Ownership and Confirmation of No Conflict**

**Employee:** Emmanuel Cohen-Yashar ("**Employee**")
**Company:** Advisor360, LLC and its affiliates ("**Company**")

---

### 1. Background

**1.1** Employee is employed by Company as a Data and AI Architect.

**1.2** Independently of that employment, Employee has conceived and is developing a personal
project described in **Schedule 1** (the "**Project**") — a software system and associated
technical design for the automated evaluation of student examination responses in primary and
secondary education, together with all documentation, source code, models, schemas, and
know-how relating to it (collectively, the "**Project IP**").

**1.3** Employee intends to license the Project IP commercially, including to one or more
academic institutions, and to receive income from doing so. Employee discloses this expressly
so that this acknowledgment is understood to cover commercial as well as personal activity.

**1.4** Employee makes this disclosure voluntarily and asks Company to confirm the matters in
Section 3.

### 2. Employee's representations

Employee represents and warrants to Company that:

**2.1 Own time.** All work on the Project has been and will be performed outside Employee's
working hours for Company and will not interfere with Employee's duties to Company.

**2.2 Own resources.** No Company equipment, network, software licence, cloud or API account,
repository, facility, or funding has been or will be used in connection with the Project.

**2.3 No Company information.** No Company confidential information, trade secret, source
code, customer data, product design, or proprietary method has been or will be used in,
incorporated into, or disclosed through the Project.

**2.4 Different field.** The Project is directed to **educational assessment and instruction**.
It is not directed to wealth management, financial advisory technology, portfolio management,
CRM, or any other line of business in which Company is engaged or, to Employee's knowledge,
has planned or announced an intention to engage.

**2.5 No customers or personnel.** Employee has not and will not solicit Company's customers,
prospects, or personnel in connection with the Project, and will not represent that Company
sponsors, endorses, or is associated with the Project.

**2.6 Continuing obligations.** Nothing in this letter relieves Employee of any confidentiality,
non-solicitation, non-competition, or other continuing obligation under Employee's employment
agreement or Company policy, all of which remain in full force.

### 3. Company's acknowledgment

In reliance on Section 2, Company acknowledges and agrees that:

**3.1 No ownership interest.** Company has no right, title, or interest in or to the Project or
the Project IP, and **the Project IP is not and will not become an assigned invention, work
made for hire, or Company property** under Employee's [*Proprietary Information and Inventions
Agreement dated [DATE]* — insert exact title and date], under any employment agreement, offer
letter, handbook, or policy, or otherwise.

**3.2 Waiver and release.** To the extent any provision of any agreement between Employee and
Company would otherwise operate to assign, license, or grant Company any interest in the
Project IP, **Company waives and releases that interest**, irrevocably and without further
consideration.

**3.3 Scope in time.** Section 3.1 and Section 3.2 apply to Project IP created **before, on, and
after** the date of this letter, including without limitation the technical design document
identified in Schedule 1, and **survive the termination of Employee's employment** for any
reason. No holdover, trailer, or post-employment assignment provision applies to the Project IP.

**3.4 No policy conflict.** Employee's development and commercial licensing of the Project, as
described in this letter and Schedule 1, does not violate Employee's employment agreement,
Company's conflict-of-interest policy, or Company's policy on outside activities, and Company
consents to it to the extent any consent is required.

**3.5 Prior inventions schedule.** This letter, together with Schedule 1, is deemed to
supplement Employee's schedule of prior and excluded inventions under the agreement named in
Section 3.1, whether or not that schedule was previously completed.

### 4. General

**4.1 No admission and no obligation.** This letter creates no obligation on Company with
respect to the Project, and Company assumes no liability arising from it. Employee bears sole
responsibility for the Project.

**4.2 Reliance.** Employee may disclose this letter to a third party contracting for the
Project IP, and to that party's counsel, solely to evidence Employee's ownership.

**4.3 Amendment.** This letter may be amended only in a writing signed by both parties.

**4.4 Governing law.** The Commonwealth of Massachusetts, without regard to conflict-of-laws
rules.

**4.5 Entire agreement on this subject.** As to the ownership of the Project IP and the
permissibility of the Project, this letter is the parties' entire agreement and supersedes any
prior oral or written understanding, including any informal approval.

### SCHEDULE 1 — Description of the Project

**Name:** Agentic Evaluation Harness for Education

**Field of use:** Automated and semi-automated evaluation of student examination responses in
primary and secondary education; teacher-facing assessment and feedback tooling.

**What it does:** Reads scanned student examination papers, evaluates written responses against
a teacher-authored marking rubric using open-weight language models, produces per-criterion
written feedback with citations to the student's own work, and routes uncertain judgments to
the teacher for review. Designed to run offline on a single machine for schools without
reliable internet connectivity.

**Foundational document:** A technical design specification titled *"Agentic Evaluation for
Education: A Research-Grounded Architecture for a Local, Open-Source Grading Harness,"* authored
by Employee, first written [MONTH YEAR], maintained in a personal repository on Employee's own
equipment.

**Development to date:** Technical design, architecture, and specification. Implementation is in
progress.

**Third parties:** Employee is in discussion with the Massachusetts Institute of Technology
Center for International Studies regarding funding of development and licensing for the
institute's research and educational use. Employee expects to retain ownership of the Project IP
under that arrangement.

**Relationship to Company's business:** None. The Project is directed at schoolteachers and
educational institutions. It does not address wealth management, financial advice, portfolio
accounting, advisor CRM, or any adjacent market.

exhibit and the cleanest route is simply to file an entry against it. It concedes less ground
than it appears to, because the operative sentence is the same.

>
> Pursuant to Section [__] of my [Proprietary Information and Inventions Agreement] dated
> [DATE], I disclose the following invention as excluded from assignment:
>
> **Invention:** "Agentic Evaluation Harness for Education" — software and technical design for
> automated evaluation of student examination responses in primary and secondary education.
>
> **Conceived:** [MONTH YEAR], on my own time and with my own equipment, without use of any
> Company resources, confidential information, or trade secrets, and outside the Company's
> field of business.
>
> **Status:** In development. I intend to license it commercially to educational and academic
> institutions.

> licensing does not conflict with the Agreement or Company policy.

## Part 4 — If Advisor360 will not sign

Do not treat silence as consent, and do not proceed on a verbal assurance.

| Response | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Signs as drafted | Clean. | Attach it to your MIT file; it is the evidence behind MIT §8.2, §13.1(c) and §17.4 |
| Signs a narrowed version — e.g. field-of-use limited to education | Good enough. | Confirm the narrowing still covers everything the MIT licence needs, and that it survives employment termination (§3.3) |
| Wants a revenue share or a licence back | They believe they have a claim. | Stop and get counsel before negotiating. Anything you concede here becomes a defect in the title you are selling MIT |
| Declines, or goes quiet | Unresolved claim. | Do **not** sign the MIT agreement. Its §8.2, §13.1(c) and §17.4 warranties would be false, exposing you to MIT *and* to your employer at once |
| Says "we don't do these" | Common and not fatal. | Ask instead for a one-line email from Legal confirming the invention is outside the scope of your assignment agreement. Weaker than a countersignature, far better than nothing |

**Sequence matters.** Resolve this **before** signing with MIT — the MIT draft's §17.4 has you
representing that performance conflicts with no third-party obligation, and you cannot make that
representation honestly while this is open. It is also, practically, the last moment when
Advisor360 has no reason to think there is money on the table worth arguing over.
